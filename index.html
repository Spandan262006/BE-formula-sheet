Module 1

Heuristics, Biases & Risk Preference

1.3 · Expected Value & Expected Utility
Expected Value
E(V) = Σ (pᵢ × xᵢ)
pᵢ = probability of outcome i · xᵢ = monetary value of outcome i. The weighted average of all possible outcomes. Econs maximize this; humans do not.
St. Petersburg Paradox — Key Insight
E(V) = ∞  ≠  Willingness to Pay
A game can have infinite expected monetary value yet humans pay only a small amount. Proves humans maximize Expected Utility E(U), not E(V). Utility is concave in wealth — diminishing marginal value.
1.3 · Loss Aversion & Prospect Theory
Loss Aversion Parameter (λ)
λ ≈ 2
Losses cause approximately twice the psychological pain of equivalent gains. Losing ₹100 hurts as much as gaining ₹200 feels good. Estimated empirically by Kahneman & Tversky.
Prospect Theory Value Function
v(x): Concave for x > 0 · Convex for x < 0
Gains domain: concave → risk-averse (prefer sure gain). Loss domain: convex → risk-seeking (prefer gamble to avoid sure loss). Reference point = 0. Developed by Kahneman & Tversky.
1.3 · Probability Weighting
Possibility Effect
w(p) > p   for small p
Very small probabilities are overweighted. A 0% → 5% jump feels disproportionately large (hope of winning). Explains lottery ticket purchases. Humans treat near-zero probabilities as if they were higher.
Certainty Effect
w(p) < p   for large p
High probabilities are underweighted relative to certainty. A 95% → 100% jump feels far more valuable than a 50% → 55% jump. The probability weighting function w(p) is S-shaped and crosses the 45° line once.
1.4 · Mental Accounting — Bundling Gains & Losses
Segregate Gains (Better)
v(x) + v(y) > v(x + y)
For x, y > 0: announcing two separate wins gives more total happiness than one combined win of the same amount. Give multiple smaller bonuses rather than one large one. Exploits concavity of value function.
Integrate Losses (Better)
v(−a) + v(−b) < v(−(a+b))
For a, b > 0: one combined loss causes less total pain than two separate losses of the same total. Deliver bad news together. Exploits convexity of value function in loss domain. e.g., v(−100)+v(−100) < v(−200).
Silver Lining Principle
v(−L) + v(+g) > v(−L + g)   if g is small
When there is a large loss L and a small gain g: segregate the gain. The small positive provides a "silver lining" that feels proportionally larger when separated. e.g., Tax refund announced separately from a bad pay month.
Transaction Utility
TU = Reference Price − Price Paid
Positive TU = you got a "deal" → more satisfaction. Negative TU = you overpaid → dissatisfaction. High MRP creates a high reference price, inflating perceived TU of discounts. Total Utility = Acquisition Utility + Transaction Utility.
Module 2

Impatience, Self-Control & Strategic Thinking

2.1 · Discounting the Future
Exponential Discounting (Samuelson — Econ Model)
U₀ = u₀ + δu₁ + δ²u₂ + ⋯ = Σᵢ₌₀^∞ δⁱuᵢ
δ (delta) = discount factor (0 < δ < 1). High δ ≈ patient; low δ ≈ impatient. Property: Dynamic Time Consistency — preferences don't reverse as time passes. Present and future selves always agree.
Beta-Delta Discounting (Hyperbolic — Human Model)
U₀ = u₀ + β(δu₁ + δ²u₂ + ⋯) = u₀ + β · Σᵢ₌₁^∞ δⁱuᵢ
β (beta) = present-bias factor (typically ≈ ½). δ = long-run patience. β multiplies ALL future utility, making the present uniquely more attractive. Causes Dynamic Time Inconsistency — preferences reverse as the present arrives.
2.1 · Self-Awareness Types (β vs. β̂)
Naif
β̂ = 1
Believes future self has no bias. Makes plans never kept. Never uses commitment devices. e.g., Gym membership never used.
Sophisticate
β̂ = β
Fully accurate self-knowledge. Proactively uses commitment devices. Plays backward induction against future self.
Partial Naif
β < β̂ < 1
Aware of some bias but underestimates severity. Takes partial corrective action. Most realistic description of real people.
2.1 · Kremer Factory Study (Dominated Contract)
Dominated Contract Structure
Pay = w/2 per unit (if output < T)  |  w per unit (if output ≥ T)
Workers voluntarily chose this over flat rate w, to self-impose discipline. Result: +2.3% production and +7% earnings. Dominated contract can improve welfare by solving the self-control problem.
Patience & Macroeconomic Wealth
↑ δ   →   ↑ log(GDP per capita)
Higher national patience (δ) correlates with higher GDP per capita, greater net adjusted savings, and more years of schooling. Patience compounds at the societal level.
2.3 · Level-k Thinking — Beauty Contest Game
Beauty Contest: Guess ⅔ of the Average (0–100)
Level k guess = (2/3)ᵏ × 50  →  0  as k → ∞
Level 0 assumes random (average = 50). Each level takes ⅔ of the previous level's guess.

2.3 · Cognitive Hierarchy Model
Cognitive Hierarchy Distribution
f(k) ~ Poisson(τ)  =  e⁻ᵗ · τᵏ / k!
τ (tau) = sophistication parameter. Higher τ → more players with higher-order reasoning. Camerer, Ho & Chong (2004). ISP Market (Goldfarb & Yang 1997): higher τ firms → higher profitability and survival rates.
Cognitive Hierarchy Belief Structure
Type-k believes others ~ Types 0 to k−1
Unlike simple Level-k, Type-k doesn't assume all others are exactly k−1; they believe opponents are distributed across all lower types. More realistic belief about the population of players.
Module 3

Nudges, Discrimination & Public Policy

3.1 · Taste-Based Discrimination (Becker)
Effective Cost of Hiring (Discriminating Employer)
Effective Wage = w* (1 + d)
w* = competitive market wage · d = psychic discrimination cost (d ≥ 0). The employer perceives hiring a minority worker as costing more than the actual wage. Higher d → fewer minority workers hired despite identical productivity.
Discrimination Cost Condition
d = 0  →  No discrimination · d > 0  →  Discrimination
When d > 0, the employer treats a minority worker as if they cost more than they do. The market wage is w*, but the employer's perceived wage is w*(1+d). Firms with lower d outcompete discriminatory firms over time.
3.1 · Key Empirical Discrimination Evidence
Goldin & Rouse — Blind Auditions
Female hiring: 10% → 35% (blind auditions)
Concealing gender of orchestra musicians increased female hiring from 10% to 35%. Shows taste-based discrimination was suppressing qualified women. Removing the observable signal (gender) eliminated the bias.
Bertrand & Mullainathan — CV Study
Callback: White name 9.65% vs. Black name 6.45%
Identical CVs with only the name changed. White-sounding names received significantly higher callback rates. Demonstrates statistical or taste-based discrimination operating purely on race-signalling name cues, not actual qualifications.
3.2 · Gender Economics — Key Quantitative Facts
Unexplained Wage Gap
~9% lower wages for women (identical qualifications)
A persistent, statistically significant wage gap remains even after controlling for education, experience, and other observable factors. This residual gap is attributed to discrimination, negotiation differences, and career interruptions.
Low Promotability Task Gap (Babcock et al.)
Women 15 pp more likely to volunteer for low-promo tasks
In mixed-gender groups. Gap disappears in single-sex groups. Driven by mutual belief that men won't volunteer, creating pressure on women to step up. Social recognition (not money) closes the gap effectively.
3.3 · Nudge Theory — Key Rules
Definition of a Nudge
Nudge: ΔBehaviour  with  ΔOptions = 0  &  ΔIncentives ≈ 0
A nudge changes behaviour without restricting choices or significantly altering economic incentives. It works through architecture — the design of how options are presented. Libertarian Paternalism: free to choose, steered toward welfare.
Default Power Rule
Default option → path of least resistance → most chosen
Defaults are the most powerful nudge tool. Auto-enrollment in pension plans dramatically increases savings rates. Humans follow the pre-set course unless they actively opt out — inertia works for policymakers.
Module 4

The Experimental Approach

4.4 · Hypothesis Testing & Statistical Analysis
Null Hypothesis & Significance Threshold
H₀: Treatment Effect = 0  ·  Reject if p < α = 0.05
Null hypothesis always states no effect and is written negatively. Reject H₀ when p-value < 0.05 (5% chance the result is random). Standard significance level in economics experiments is α = 0.05.
Choosing the Right Statistical Test
Continuous variable → t-test  ·  Binary variable → χ² test
t-test: for continuous outcomes (income, test scores, weight). Chi-Square (χ²) test: for binary/categorical outcomes (donated yes/no, voted yes/no). Applies to both Balance Tables and Summary Tables.
4.4 · Experimental Design Requirements
Balance Table Logic
Balance Table: Compare covariates BEFORE treatment
Checks that treatment & control groups have statistically similar baseline characteristics (age, gender, income). If groups are balanced, randomization worked. Any significant difference = failed randomization = invalid experiment.
Summary Table Logic
Summary Table: Compare outcomes AFTER treatment
Compares outcome variables between treatment and control groups. The difference in means = estimated causal treatment effect. Used to determine whether the intervention had a statistically significant impact.
4.3 · IRB & Ethics Requirements (Quantitative Facts)
IRB Minimum Size
Min. 5 members
Institutional Review Board must have at least 5 members to review human-subjects research protocols.
Data Retention
Minimum 3 years
Research data must be securely retained for at least 3 years after study completion before destruction.
Significance Level
α = 0.05 (95% CI)
Standard threshold in economics. p < 0.05 → statistically significant result → reject H₀.

Mental Accounting — When to Bundle vs. Separate

When to Segregate (Separate)
Multiple Gains  |  Large Loss + Small Gain
Multiple gains: v(x) + v(y) > v(x+y) → announce separately.
Silver lining: Segregate a small gain from a large loss for psychological comfort.
When to Integrate (Combine)
Multiple Losses  |  Large Gain + Small Loss
Multiple losses: v(−a)+v(−b) < v(−(a+b)) → deliver bad news together.
Cancel losses: Large gain offsets a small loss better when combined.
